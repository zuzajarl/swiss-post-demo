# Branch network development 2026–2028 — programme knowledge

> **Note for the knowledge base:** this file answers questions *about the programme*.
> Questions about a *specific location* — status, closure date, address, opening hours,
> nearest alternative — are handled by the tools, not by this file.
>
> **Demonstration data.** The programme figures match the publicly communicated scale.

## Scope of the programme

By 2028, **170 branches across 155 municipalities** will be converted or closed.

| Year | Branches | Status |
|------|----------|--------|
| 2025 | 28 | already implemented |
| 2026 | 46 | under way |
| 2027 | 58 | planned |
| 2028 | 38 | planned, partly still under discussion |

The programme affects roughly one sixth of the network. The large majority of locations
stays unchanged.

---

## What "conversion" actually means

An affected branch does not simply end. There are four possible outcomes:

**Partner branch.** The postal counter moves into an existing shop in the same place —
Coop, Volg, Landi, Denner or a local store. Letters, parcels, payments and cash withdrawal
remain available. Opening hours are usually considerably longer than at the previous
branch, often from seven in the morning to eight in the evening, and all day Saturday.

**Parcel pickup point.** A staffed point for collecting and dropping off parcels. No
payments, no cash.

**My Post 24.** A parcel terminal, accessible around the clock, seven days a week,
including public holidays.

**Home service.** The carrier handles counter business at the front door. For people who
cannot reach an access point within reasonable distance, the home service is free.

---

## Commitments

**Universal service.** Access to the universal postal service remains guaranteed in every
municipality. No municipality loses access entirely.

**Reachability.** For 90 percent of the population, an access point is reachable within 20
minutes on foot or by public transport.

**Consultation.** Every affected municipality is consulted before a conversion. Discussions
begin at least six months before the effective date. Municipalities can comment on the
proposed replacement location as part of that process.

**Employees.** Affected employees are offered continued employment within the company.

---

## Common objections and the factual answer

**"This is a dismantling of universal service."**
The statutory universal service mandate is unchanged and continues to be met. What changes
is the form of the access point, not the access itself. In many places the new opening
hours are longer than the old ones.

**"Older people won't cope with this."**
That is precisely what the home service is for: the carrier accepts items at the front
door, sells stamps and delivers cash. It is free for people with no reachable access point.
Nobody is required to use a smartphone.

**"Why wasn't our municipality asked?"**
Every affected municipality is consulted before the decision, with at least six months'
notice. If a caller says they heard nothing about it, the point should be passed to the
responsible office.

**"Can the decision be reversed?"**
Swiss Post decides on individual locations after consulting the municipality. The voice
agent takes no position on this and makes no commitments. Political requests are handed
over to the responsible office.

## What the agent does not handle

- Personnel questions about individual employees
- Political assessments of the programme or positions on parliamentary motions
- Promises that a given location will be kept or reopened
- Shipment tracking, complaints, account or payment questions

In these cases: briefly explain that another office is responsible, and offer the handover.
